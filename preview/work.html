Chapter 1: Nowhere Left To Go Back To

Chapter Text

24 July 1991

Privet Drive prided itself on regularity. It was the sort of street that mistook predictability for virtue and conformity for safety, where the hedges were trimmed to identical heights and the curtains were drawn each evening at precisely the same hour, as though deviation itself might invite catastrophe. Sound, here, was expected to behave. Engines did not idle. Footsteps did not linger.

Harry had learned to live inside that silence.

He did so carefully, the way one learns to navigate a house riddled with loose floorboards: by memorizing where weight could safely fall, and where even the smallest misstep would announce itself. He had been living with his aunt—his mother’s sister, Petunia Dursley—since before he could remember, though she rarely acknowledged the connection except when it served as explanation for why he was there at all.

That afternoon, Harry was in the kitchen, drying plates no one had asked him to wash, because experience had taught him that usefulness was a kind of camouflage. Vernon sat at the table, his newspaper spread wide, elbows planted as though staking a claim.

The owl passed without announcement, its shadow sliding briefly across the frosted glass above the sink. Harry felt it more than he saw it—a subtle tightening in his chest, a wrongness in the air, like the moment just before a storm breaks.

Mr. Harry James Potter
The Cupboard Under the Stairs
4 Privet Drive
Little Whinging, Surrey

For a moment, Harry could not move. The cupboard under the stairs was not an address. It was a fact of humiliation, a space defined by what it was not meant to be.

And for the first time in his life, Harry did not know whether that was something to fear… or something to protect at all costs.
